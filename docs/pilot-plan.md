# Four-week learning pilot

Start after a speaker has approved the course being tested and its reference audio is available. The current preview is suitable for usability feedback, not a claim of language-teaching efficacy.

Recruit 30–50 consenting learners from the intended audience. Include beginners in Twi, Fante and Kasem; keep each language's results separate. With a small sample, treat findings as direction rather than proof. Do not send invitations or collect recordings without the participants' agreement.

## Before the pilot

- Have reviewers finish the content packet and check the app on an Android phone.
- Ask each learner why they want to learn and how much they already know.
- Test a few course expressions and a short spoken exchange, scored by a qualified speaker. Use a comparable but different prompt at the end.
- Choose the primary measure in advance: ability to recall and use the taught expressions after four weeks. XP and lesson completion alone are not learning measures.

## Observe the first session

Record whether the learner reaches the first teaching card, completes the first lesson, and knows what to do next. Note confusion, missing audio, microphone permission problems, and any unfamiliar spelling. Ask them to describe what the lesson taught them.

## Follow up after one week and four weeks

Ask whether they are still using the app, which days they practised, and what made them return or stop. Count day-seven and day-28 return rates against the original recruited cohort, including people who stop responding. Report uncertainty and missing responses.

Check delayed recall of a small fixed set of taught expressions without hints. Have a speaker evaluate the follow-up exchange for understandable use of the expressions. Ask which parts of the app they would recommend and whether any paid offering would be worthwhile; no payment is required for participation.

The app currently stores progress locally and has no new analytics service. Use consented observation and interviews for the pilot. The progress screen distinguishes introduced, practising and remembered expressions, but it does not measure speaking proficiency. “Remembered” requires three successful scheduled typed reviews on separate days.

## Decide what to improve

If learners struggle in the first lesson, fix teaching and wording before adding content. If they complete lessons but cannot recall expressions later, adjust practice and review intervals. If they learn but stop returning, use interviews to investigate the daily experience. Correct speaker-reported content errors before expanding the course.

Do not generalise one language's results to the other two. Fante and Kasem currently have smaller content sets and deliberately revisit more expressions; compare learning outcomes, not just lesson counts.
